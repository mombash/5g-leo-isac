# Scientific attribution and adaptations

The comparison receivers implement or adapt the methods below. They are research implementations for the NR observation model in this repository, not distributions of the cited authors' code.

- **Two-step detection.** Grossi, Lops and Venturino, “Two-Step Sequential Detection in Agile-Beam Radars: Performance and Tradeoffs,” *IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems* 53(5), 2199–2213 (2017), DOI [10.1109/TAES.2017.2688878](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.2017.2688878). The fixed-look and early-look configurations are operating points of this family. The implementation adapts calibration, motion-cell propagation and delay–Doppler refinement. A second look uses fresh samples and preserves unresolved-cell identity.
- **Dual-scale search.** Li et al., “Efficient Dual-Scale Generalized Radon-Fourier Transform Detector Family for Long Time Coherent Integration,” *IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing* (2024), DOI [10.1109/TSP.2024.3411161](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSP.2024.3411161). Coarse migration correction is reused across fine Doppler-rate trials. The implementation uses bistatic centered-baseband observations, all 27 permitted rates and the common local refinement.
- **Hybrid integration.** He, Yang and Chen, “A Hybrid Integration Method for Moving Target Detection With GNSS-Based Passive Radar,” *IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Applied Earth Observations and Remote Sensing* 14, 1184–1193 (2021), DOI [10.1109/JSTARS.2020.3037200](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSTARS.2020.3037200). Keystone alignment and Lv’s distribution are adapted to one second of NR samples with positive-lag finite discretization and peak interpolation. GNSS-specific processing and integration across multiple one-second observations are omitted. An independently calibrated maximum-score threshold replaces the source's constant-false-alarm-rate detector. Finite-boundary and discretization choices limit equivalence to the published GNSS receiver.
- **Adaptive information acquisition.** The selector implementation draws on established acquisition and neural/statistical modeling techniques. Its source attribution includes Covert et al., ICML 2023, [primary paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/covert23a/covert23a.pdf).

MATLAB and 5G Toolbox generate the configured NR waveforms. PyTorch, Triton, NumPy, SciPy and their dependencies retain their own licenses. They are installed separately. Distribution terms for this repository are described in [RIGHTS.md](RIGHTS.md).
